LabFlow design system

LabFlow lets researchers ask CS and engineering papers questions and check every answer against the page it came from. The look is paper and ink, with one green that means verified. It should feel calm, exact, and quietly confident, like a well-set paper. The promise is the tagline: Answers you can verify.

03

Color

Paper and ink carry the interface. One green means verified, one orange means needs you, and every color has a light and a dark value.

Rules

From the brand book
  • Paper and ink carry the interface. paper is the ground; surface is the panels on it, with a line border; ink is the text; ink-muted is everything secondary.
  • accent means verified: primary actions, links, citation chips, the running and done status, and the evidence outline in the PDF. Keep it off decoration and large backgrounds, and use one primary button per view.
  • attention and attention-text mean needs you: failed papers and errors only. Orange is the only warm color in the system.
  • Mark a cited region of a PDF page with an evidence fill and a 1.5px evidence-line outline. PDF pages stay white in both themes, so these two keep their light values in the dark theme; only the stage around the pages (surface-sunken) darkens.
  • Put on-accent on accent fills, never a literal white; ink and accent both read on accent-soft.
  • Support the light and dark themes from the start: every color here has both values.

04

Type

Three families with three jobs: DM Sans for what you operate, Source Serif 4 for what you read, DM Mono for what you copy or cite.

Rules

From the brand book
  • Set the interface in DM Sans, the logo’s family. Use body by default, label for controls, caption for metadata, heading and title for structure, display only for page titles, and overline (uppercase) for group labels.
  • Set answers and quoted passages in Source Serif 4 (answer, passage), so what LabFlow writes reads like the paper it came from.
  • Use DM Mono (code, cite) for things you could copy or cite: commands, ids, model names, and the page in a citation chip.

05

Layout and shape

A 4px grid, soft corners, and borders instead of shadows.

Rules

From the brand book
  • Space on the 4px grid: space-2 inside chips, space-3 inside buttons and inputs, space-4 for card padding, space-5 between groups, space-6 page margins, space-7 between page regions.
  • Use borders, not shadows. Panels and cards are surface with a line border and radius-lg. Only floating things (menus, popovers, the citation preview) get shadow-pop.
  • Use radius-sm for badges and small chips, radius-md for buttons and inputs, radius-lg for cards and panels, radius-xl for the sign-in card and large figures, and radius-full for citation chips and status pills.
  • Give every interactive element a keyboard focus state: a solid 2px focus-ring outline at a 2px offset.

07

Citations

Every claim points to a page, and the page shows the passage.

Rules

From the brand book
  • A citation chip is DM Mono cite text in accent on accent-soft, with radius-full: p. 7. It sits inline at the end of the sentence it supports.
  • Selecting a chip fills it with accent (text on-accent), scrolls the PDF to that page, and draws the evidence highlight around the passage with a “Cited in answer 1 · p. 7” tag.
  • An answer with no support shows no chips: it says “Not in this paper.” in answer style, then lists the closest passages in passage style.
